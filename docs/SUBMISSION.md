**Project name:** C-Sync
**High-Level Summary (draft):** C-Sync is an offline-first assistant that helps a deep-space crew notice unusual changes in their health and performance, guides them step by step through a medical emergency, and, under the demo's rules, refuses to resume work when the supporting data are stale, with a ground delay of up to about 20 minutes or no link at all. It compares each astronaut to their own baseline, shows its confidence, flags missing data as unknown instead of normal, and leaves the final decision to a human operator. It uses real NASA ISS cabin telemetry (OSDR Environmental Data App) and labelled synthetic crew data.

**Project Details (draft, M5 verifies against README.md before pasting):**
C-Sync (Care Sync) is an offline-first assistant for a crew far from Earth, where a message can take up to about 20 minutes each way and links can drop for long periods. It runs on the crew's side and does three things.
1. *Notice.* It compares each astronaut with their own baseline (first five days) instead of a universal norm. A dimension is flagged when it drifts beyond 1.5 standard deviations for two consecutive days. Body, Attention and Morale are evaluated; Mission and Habitat are shown as context. States: NORMAL, WATCH, DEGRADED, UNKNOWN.
2. *Guide.* On an emergency event (in our story, chest pain) it opens a fixed procedure, guides the measurements one by one, records each value with its time and origin, and queues the report for the ground; the queue deduplicates by message ID. It proposes no treatment or drug.
3. *Protect the decision.* A resume gate refuses to resume work when the last measurement is too old (limit: 10 minutes) and explains the reason in plain words. A commander override is logged but is not enough on its own in the implemented rules.
Everything the demo shows is computed from a labelled synthetic crew (generator csync-synth-0.1, seed 42). The only real data are NASA ISS cabin telemetry (OSDR Environmental Data App, mission RR-1), used for context (CO2 against the NASA-STD-3001 requirement) and data-quality checks. On synthetic scenarios, with the threshold tuned on those same scenarios, 0.7 % of 300 healthy crews triggered a procedure, 95.3 % of gradual drifts and 59.3 % of abrupt jumps were detected. Nothing is clinically validated. Stack: Python, NumPy, pandas, Streamlit, jsonschema, pytest (174 tests). Goal: an explainable, testable engine where the final decision stays with the crew and the crew medical officer.
